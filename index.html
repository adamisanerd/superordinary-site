02 / behind the songs

the story

superordinary is a solo melodic alternative-rock project built around a simple idea: the extraordinary presence of God can be found in ordinary life.

It grew out of a feeling I've carried most of my life: being the one who gets passed over. The last one picked, the first to miss out, watching the blessings land on everyone else. These songs are about learning that grace doesn't skip the overlooked. It goes looking for them.

Drawing on the guitar-driven alternative rock of the late ’90s and the melodic, piano-rich indie rock of the early 2000s, the songs explore faith, doubt, nostalgia, redemption, and the beauty of being human.

ordinary people. extraordinary grace.

001 / the beginning

why superordinary?

Notes from the one who usually gets overlooked.

read note +

I've always been the one who gets overlooked.
Last one picked. First one to miss out.

I watch the blessings land on everyone around me and I wonder, again, why not me.

For a long time I quietly believed God had passed me over too. That I was the one He forgot.

But I keep reading about people nobody noticed. A shepherd kid left out in the field while his brothers lined up. A woman drawing water at noon so no one would see her. Two tired travelers on the road to Emmaus who didn't recognize who was walking with them.

Grace found every one of them. Right there in the ordinary.

I don't have this figured out. Some days I'm still at the bottom of the wheel, waiting.

But maybe being overlooked by everyone isn't the same as being overlooked by Him.

Maybe that's what superordinary means.

002 / ep, track 01

something worth dying for

The kingdom I was building was me.

read note +

The kingdom was me.

I can see it clearly now, the way you only see a house for what it was after it's burned down. Every room was built for one person. I was selfish. Self-centered. I didn't care about anyone but me, and I didn't care who got hurt.

People got hurt. I'd watch their names light up my phone and let it ring.

It came from my own hurt. That's not an excuse, just the truth. Hurt people hurt people, and I got very good at it.

I remember the night it finally caved in. Sitting in my truck in an empty parking lot with the engine off, because there was nowhere I wanted to go and nobody left I hadn't pushed away.

That's where He found me. Not at my best. At the bottom of everything I'd built.

He called me by another name.
Not selfish. Not the guy who hurts people. Not the names my hurt gave me.
Son.

It wasn't the nails that held Him there. It was a love that wouldn't let me go, even while I was the one doing the hurting.

He didn't wait for me to become someone worth saving.

I had nothing left to live for.
He gave me something worth dying for.

003 / ep, track 02

the end of me

Everyone believed it. God knew better.

read note +

I grew up knowing exactly what to say and do.

Third pew from the front. Hand up at the right part of the song. I knew which verses to quote and when to bow my head. I could pray out loud in a circle and make it sound like I meant every word.

I walked the walk. Talked the talk. Everyone believed it.

It was an act.

The second the car doors shut in the parking lot, I took it off like a Sunday shirt.

Nobody saw the real me. Not my friends. Not the people who said they were proud of me. Some days, not even me.

But He did. The whole time. He saw the kid mouthing the words and the man still hiding behind them, and that's exactly the one He wanted. Not a better performance. A real relationship.

I always thought He was waiting for me to finally get it right. Arms crossed at the finish line.

He wasn't waiting.
He was walking toward me the whole time.

The end of the act.
The start of something real.

004 / ep, track 03

buried underneath this town

All the things I couldn't keep.

read note +

Went back today.
Took the long way, past the water tower and the ball field with the lights that never all worked.

The corner store is a vape shop now. Half the field is a parking lot. Our sidewalk is still there, though. It's cracked down the middle, and half our names are worn away under twenty-some summers of other people walking over them.

We carved them with a stick the afternoon they poured it. Somebody's dad yelled at us from a porch. We ran, laughing so hard we couldn't breathe, and I remember thinking nothing would ever change.

Everything changed.

There's a picture of me from that summer: sunburned, missing a tooth, grinning like I already knew how it all turned out. I wonder what he'd think of me now. Whether I kept any of the promises he didn't know he was making.

I've spent so much of my life trying to dig things back up. Old summers. Old versions of me. People who moved on.

But I'm starting to think some things get buried for a reason.
Not lost. Planted.

All the things I couldn't keep.
But He kept me.
