02 / behind the songs

the story

there’s nothing particularly remarkable about being ordinary.

most of life happens somewhere between what we hoped for and what actually happens. opportunities come and go. doors open for some and close for others. and sometimes, no matter how prepared, capable, or qualified someone might be, they’re simply not considered.

there’s a particular kind of disappointment in that. not being passed over because you weren’t good enough, but because you weren’t thought of at all.

it’s easy to watch other people receive the opportunities you’ve worked toward and begin to wonder what makes them different. sometimes the answer isn’t anything obvious. sometimes there’s no answer at all.

and that’s where things get complicated.

because disappointment can quietly become comparison. comparison becomes envy. and before long, the desire to be included turns into the belief that something is owed.

it’s a difficult balance, knowing you have something worthwhile to offer while recognizing that your worth was never supposed to depend on whether anyone noticed.

there’s a story in john 5 about a man who had spent thirty-eight years waiting beside a pool. when Jesus asked if he wanted to be healed, the man explained that every time he tried to reach the water, someone else got there first.

there’s something familiar about that.

but Jesus didn’t ask him to prove he deserved a turn. He didn’t compare him to the people who’d gone before him. He saw him where he was.

maybe that’s what makes the story so meaningful.

being overlooked doesn’t mean being unworthy. being qualified doesn’t guarantee being chosen. and watching someone else receive an opportunity doesn’t mean God has somehow withheld His goodness.

none of that makes disappointment easy. the temptation to compare, to question, or to feel entitled doesn’t simply disappear. faith doesn’t always silence those thoughts, either.

but it offers another way to see them.

a reminder that God’s love isn’t earned through accomplishment, recognition, or being the person everyone remembers. that His intentions are always good even when circumstances seem unfair. and that an ordinary life isn’t somehow less meaningful because it doesn’t look extraordinary to everyone else.

that’s where superordinary comes from.

it’s music about disappointment and expectation, about the struggle between selfish ambition and genuine faith, and about learning to recognize grace in a life that doesn’t always go according to plan.

it’s about knowing there’s something worthwhile to offer without needing the world to confirm it.

and believing that even when no one else seems to notice, God still sees.

ordinary people. extraordinary grace.john 5:1–9
